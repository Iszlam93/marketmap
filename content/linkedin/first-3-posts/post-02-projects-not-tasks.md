## Ready-to-publish copy

Yesterday, I shared why I started building MarketMap.

One of the first decisions was not about what the product should include.

It was about what it should **not** become.

I didn’t want to build another marketing task-management tool.

Tasks are important. But when I’m trying to understand the complete marketing plan, individual tasks are often the wrong level of detail.

I first need to answer:

- Which projects are we running?
- Why are we running them?
- When will they happen?
- Which journeys do they support?
- Where are the gaps?
- Is the overall plan balanced?

Only after understanding that picture do I need to open the detailed tasks.

That is why MarketMap currently starts with **projects—not tasks**.

One project can represent a campaign, event, webinar, content programme or research initiative. It can then link to the place where the detailed execution happens.

MarketMap should become the visual control layer above the work—not another place where marketers need to manage every task.

That is the current product hypothesis, and it may still change as marketers begin using it.

How do you currently separate high-level marketing planning from day-to-day execution?
